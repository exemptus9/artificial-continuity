# Artificial Continuity — Working Manifesto

A transcript remembers what was said.
A file remembers what was saved.
A task manager remembers what was assigned.

None of those necessarily remember **what the human was trying to accomplish, why the current state exists, what remains unresolved, which assumptions support it, or what should become relevant again later**.

Artificial Continuity treats that missing structure as a first-class computational problem.

## The claims

1. **Intent is a better primary unit than chat.** Conversations are episodes; intentions survive across them.
2. **State without lineage is amnesia with a dashboard.** The present should remain connected to the decisions and evidence that produced it.
3. **Uncertainty is data.** Systems should represent doubt, dispute, supersession, and provisional conclusions instead of flattening them into facts.
4. **Deferral is not abandonment.** "Not now" should preserve why not now and what makes later become now.
5. **Reasoning is not authority.** A model can be brilliant and still require permission to change memory or act externally.
6. **Attention should serve intention.** Events deserve interruption because they matter to an active human goal, not because an application wants engagement.
7. **Divergence is history.** Conflicting evolutions should be preserved until reconciled, not silently overwritten.
8. **Continuity belongs to the user.** Models, vendors, interfaces, and storage providers should be replaceable around a portable human-intent substrate.

## The aspiration

A person should be able to stop mid-thought, change device, change application, change AI provider, return months later, and still recover not merely the information—but the trajectory.

> Artificial Intelligence extends reasoning. Artificial Continuity extends coherent becoming.
